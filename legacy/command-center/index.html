A collections desk that checks its own math.

    What the lead sees at 6:30 in the morning

    One brief built from every sheet: money due today, deadlines, accounts still ordering while late, and what to raise in the meeting. It arrives by email and lives in the Owner_Today tab.

    Meeting agenda, built automatically

    The emails, exactly as they are sent

    Every agent starts the day with a ranked list

    Each account earns points for age, size, a promise due today, a late follow-up, new orders while 60+ days late and more. The list is cut at the agent's daily capacity, and every row says why it is there and what to do.

    Open queries and escalations from other teams

    Read from the shared request sheet and the escalation sheet. Due dates come from the sheet, or from a service level by request type; owners get "due today" and "did you work on this?" reminders.

    Accounts that need a decision above the desk

    Routes: resolve the dispute first, review open credits, demand letter, small claims, write-off review, or a credit hold for accounts that keep ordering while late. The system recommends; a person decides.

    How it stays accurate
    
    
    Spreadsheets fail silently, so every run proves itself. If any blocking check fails, nothing is published and yesterday's lists stay live.

        One Apps Script engine
        • Reads every source by file ID, never edits it
        • Cleans, joins into one row per account
        • Scores, ranks, routes and sizes limits
        • Reconciles to the cent
        • Publishes, emails, drafts documents
        
      
        ›
        
      
        What people use
        • Owner brief and Ask tab
        • One worklist file per agent
        • Management review and credit review
        • Chargeback work queue and drafts
        • A management workbook with no customer data
        
    
        
    
        Everything runs inside the company's own Google account: Sheets, Apps Script, Gmail and Drive, at no added cost. Credit limits, data fixes and reassignments change only when a person ticks approve, and each decision is logged with a name and a time.
        
    
        The engine is about 1,700 lines of plain JavaScript with 44 automated tests built on hand-calculated examples. The same code runs in the browser for this page and in Apps Script for the live workbooks.
